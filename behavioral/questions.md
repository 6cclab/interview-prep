# Behavioral Question Bank

Phrasings grouped by competency, for `/mock` to select from. See
`behavioral/competencies.md` for what each competency is actually testing —
read that before answering, not the question in isolation.

## Spotting the competency behind unfamiliar phrasing

Interviewers rarely name the competency. Use these tells:

- **"Tell me about a time you..." + a named emotion or reaction** ("...you
  were frustrated", "...you disagreed") almost always maps to conflict,
  disagreeing-up, or working-with-product-design depending on who the other
  party is.
- **"...when the requirements weren't clear" / "...you had to figure out
  what to build"** → operating under ambiguity, sometimes overlapping with
  setting technical direction if the answer is really about deciding, not
  just discovering.
- **"...something went wrong" / "...you'd do differently"** → failure. If
  it's specifically about an outage or a customer-facing break, it's the
  incident question instead.
- **"...you had to convince people" / "...without formal authority"** →
  influence without authority. If the people being convinced are the
  candidate's own reports, it's not this — redirect mentally to mentoring or
  direction-setting.
- **"...a decision you didn't agree with"** → check who made the decision.
  A peer → conflict. Someone above the candidate → disagreeing-up, and the
  interviewer almost always cares more about the "after" than the
  disagreement itself.
- **"...grew someone" / "...helped someone improve"** → mentoring, even when
  phrased as a leadership question rather than a people-development one.
- **"...had to say no" / "...pushed back on a request" / "...too much on
  your plate"** → prioritisation, unless the requester is specifically
  product or design, in which case it may be intended as that competency
  instead — listen for which one the interviewer follows up on.
- **"...noticed a pattern" / "...kept happening" / "...root cause across"**
  → escalating a systemic problem. The words "kept happening" or "again" are
  the strongest signal.
- **"...why are you looking" / "...why us"** is always the leaving/this-company
  question regardless of how it's dressed up.

When two competencies both seem plausible, answer the one the follow-up
question is clearly steering toward — the first question is often broad on
purpose.

## Conflict / disagreement with a peer

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague at your level. How did
  you resolve it?
- Describe a situation where you and another engineer had fundamentally
  different opinions on a technical approach. What happened?
- Give me an example of a time you had to work through a conflict with a
  peer. What was the outcome?
- Tell me about a disagreement you had with someone you had no authority
  over. How did you handle it?
- Walk me through a time you pushed back on another engineer's design. Did
  it change anything?
- Have you ever had a strong disagreement with a coworker that got personal
  or tense? What did you do?

## Disagreeing with a decision from above, and what you did after

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision made by your manager or
  a leader. What did you do?
- Describe a time leadership chose a direction you thought was wrong. How did
  you respond once the decision was made?
- Give me an example of when you had to execute on something you didn't
  agree with.
- Tell me about a time you were overruled. What happened next?
- Walk me through a situation where you raised a concern about a decision
  and it didn't change the outcome.
- Have you ever had to deliver on a plan you thought was a mistake? How did
  you show up for it?

## Failure

- Tell me about a time you failed. What happened and what did you learn?
- Describe a mistake you made that had real consequences.
- Give me an example of a decision you made that you'd take back if you
  could.
- Tell me about a project that didn't go the way you expected. What was your
  role in that?
- What's something you got wrong professionally, and how did it change how
  you work now?
- Walk me through a time your judgment was off. How did you find out, and
  what did you do about it?

## Influence without authority

- Tell me about a time you had to convince people you had no authority over
  to do something differently.
- Describe a situation where you drove a change across teams without being
  asked to.
- Give me an example of getting buy-in for an idea when you weren't the
  decision-maker.
- Tell me about a time you had to build consensus among people who didn't
  report to you and didn't have to listen to you.
- Walk me through how you got an initiative adopted outside your own team.
- Have you ever changed how a group of people worked without having formal
  say over them?

## Operating under ambiguity

- Tell me about a time you had to make progress with an unclear or
  incomplete problem statement.
- Describe a situation where the requirements kept changing or were never
  fully defined. How did you handle it?
- Give me an example of a project where you had to figure out what to build,
  not just how.
- Tell me about a time you had to make a call with incomplete information.
- Walk me through how you approached a problem nobody had fully scoped yet.
- How do you operate when priorities or direction aren't clear? Give a
  specific example.

## Mentoring and growing other engineers

- Tell me about a time you helped another engineer grow.
- Describe a situation where you mentored someone through a specific gap in
  their skills or confidence.
- Give me an example of developing talent on your team or in your org.
- Tell me about someone whose trajectory you meaningfully changed. What did
  you actually do?
- Walk me through how you approach giving feedback to someone more junior
  than you.
- Have you ever had to let someone struggle with something instead of
  stepping in? Why, and what happened?

## Setting technical direction

- Tell me about a time you set the technical direction for a project or
  system.
- Describe a situation where you had to choose between multiple viable
  technical approaches. How did you decide?
- Give me an example of a technical decision you made that other teams had
  to build around.
- Tell me about a time you changed the trajectory of a system or codebase.
- Walk me through how you got a group of engineers aligned on an approach
  when they started out with different opinions.
- Have you ever had to define an architecture with long-term consequences?
  What was the process?

## Escalating a systemic problem rather than patching the local instance

- Tell me about a time you noticed a problem was part of a bigger pattern
  rather than a one-off.
- Describe a situation where a quick fix was available but you pushed for a
  structural one instead.
- Give me an example of an incident or bug that turned out to be a symptom
  of something larger.
- Tell me about a recurring issue you finally got to the root of.
- Walk me through a time you escalated something beyond your own team
  because the fix needed to happen somewhere else.
- Have you ever had to make the case that a "small" problem was actually
  worth fixing at the root? What convinced people?

## Prioritisation and saying no

- Tell me about a time you had to say no to a request from a stakeholder.
- Describe a situation where you had to cut scope under time pressure. How
  did you decide what to cut?
- Give me an example of pushing back on a deadline or a commitment that
  wasn't realistic.
- Tell me about a time you had too much on your plate and had to decide what
  not to do.
- Walk me through how you prioritize when multiple important things compete
  for your time.
- Have you ever told someone their request wasn't going to happen? How did
  you handle it?

## Handling a production incident

- Tell me about a time something broke in production. Walk me through what
  you did.
- Describe the worst incident you've been part of. What was your role?
- Give me an example of debugging something under real time pressure.
- Tell me about a time you had to make a call between a fast mitigation and
  a proper fix during an outage.
- Walk me through how you communicated during an incident where other people
  or customers were affected.
- What changed after your last serious incident? What did you personally do
  about it?

## Working with product and design

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product or design decision.
- Describe a situation where a spec had a technical cost the requester
  didn't see. What did you do?
- Give me an example of shaping a product or design decision before it
  became a commitment.
- Tell me about a time you had to push back on a PM or designer without
  damaging the relationship.
- Walk me through a project where engineering, product, and design had to
  work through real tension to land on scope.
- Have you ever changed a product's direction because of something you
  raised as an engineer?

## Why you're leaving / why this company

- Why are you looking to leave your current role?
- What's prompting the change right now?
- Why this company, specifically?
- What are you looking for that you're not getting today?
- How does this role fit into where you want your career to go next?
- What would need to be true for this to be the right move for you?
